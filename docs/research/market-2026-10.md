# Bilingual reading apps: market research (2026-10-06)

## Takeaways
- **Mobile winners** use tap-a-word to open a bottom sheet (short meaning first), long-press or drag to select a phrase or sentence, and progressive reveal (word, then sentence, then paragraph), with audio within thumb reach.
- **Desktop winners** use hover lookups, drag to select a phrase (Readlang), keyboard shortcuts (Lute), and wide layouts: the translation under each paragraph (Immersive Translate) or two synced columns.
- **Word status tracking** (LingQ and Lute colour unknown, learning and known words) is the main learning feature. Immersive Translate has the best layout but no learning features.
- **Cost:** a whole novel costs about $0.10 to $1.50 with a small LLM and about $12 with Google. Incumbents charge $10 to $15 a month.
- **Gaps:** nothing combines your own EPUBs, context-aware LLM translation, word tracking, good phrase selection and mobile/desktop sync. Translation density that fades as you improve is also missing, and explanations in Portuguese are rare.
- Reddit was blocked from the research environment, so user sentiment comes from App Store, add-on store and forum reviews.

## 4. User sentiment (themes)

**Praise**
- **Fluid reading with one tap.** "The way you can read a book so fluidly and tap on any word for instant translation is wonderful" (Linga). "Best app interface for reading a language you are trying to learn" (Beelinguapp).
- **A calm interface.** "No distracting noises or cartoons flying around the screen" (Linga). Readlang users chose it "to get away from the multicoloured text" of LingQ.
- **Native audio synced to the text**, especially Beelinguapp's karaoke mode and Du Chinese's narration.
- **Human-quality graded content** (Du Chinese, Readle), which is why they rate 4.7–4.9.
- **LLM explanations.** On Readlang: "The reading on here is just so much smoother… especially with the explain feature."

**Complaints**
- **Bad translations.** On LingQ: "some are just plain wrong", and AI output for Japanese and Polish is "unusable". Beelinguapp's AI stories should be checked by "someone who spoke the language".
- **Paywalls and dark patterns.** LingQ's free tier gives only 20 LingQs, its cancellation flow is "disrespectful", and users report unwanted charges. duoBooks' free limits are unclear.
- **Gamification noise.** LingQ users complain of "popups and incentives…reminders to keep up your streak that are always in the way".
- **Multi-word selection is hard on a phone** (LingQ, per Trustpilot). Re-tapping in Readlang deletes the translation instead of showing it again.
- **No memory of what you looked up.** Readlang users want earlier translations to persist or show on hover. Beelinguapp has no SRS. Users want flashcards fed straight from their reading.
- **Review overload.** LingQ showed 171 cards pending after one lesson.
- **The best tools are desktop-only.** Language Reactor's reading mode and Readlang's full UI are not on phones, and users ask for them.
- **Dictionaries fall back to English.** LingQ shows English definitions even when the target language is set as the definition language. Portuguese speakers often get English as the bridge language (inferred from the language lists).

## 5. Mobile UX patterns that work

1. **Tap word → bottom sheet or anchored popover**, not a modal and not a page navigation. Show the gloss first, with an expand option for grammar, AI explanation and audio. Dismiss by tapping the text again or swiping down. Kindle cards, LingQ popups and Apple's Translate sheet all use this pattern.
2. **Long-press, or drag with handles, to select a phrase or sentence.** This is the main pain point. Snap the selection to word and sentence boundaries. A **double-tap or a sentence-number button** can translate the whole sentence.
3. **Graduated reveal**, in order: inline gloss, then the sentence translation, then the parallel paragraph. Side-by-side columns are cramped on a phone, so Beelinguapp and SmartBook stack them or show them per paragraph, and Ember has a "meaning-first" toggle. **Interlinear** text (a gloss under each word or line) suits small screens if it is toggleable.
4. **Audio synced at the word or sentence level, with a mini-player** within thumb reach at the bottom. The current sentence is highlighted and there is a speed control (Beelinguapp, Polylingo, LingQ, Readlang's MP3 sync).
5. **Sentence mode** (LingQ): one sentence per screen, swiped through. Good for one-handed reading and pairs well with sentence translation.
6. **Word-status colouring, made optional.** It motivates some users and annoys others. The Readlang forum consensus is that it must be possible to turn it off.
7. **Saved words stay marked in the text** (LinguaRead) and **reveal their saved gloss on a light tap**, with no new lookup.
8. **Offline first.** Downloaded books, local dictionaries and on-device MT or LLMs (Ember, Polylingo). Commuters read on the subway.
9. **Lifetime or low-priced plans**, which SmartBook, Linga and Lingo Books all offer, are popular given subscription fatigue.

## 6. Gaps in the market (opportunities)

- **Your own EPUB, LLM context translation, synced audio and good phrase selection all in one polished mobile app does not exist.** LingQ has the features but a cluttered UI and an expensive plan. The calm apps (Readlang, Language Reactor) are weak or missing on mobile. The import-focused apps (SmartBook, Linga, LinguaRead, Ember) are small and new.
- **Portuguese (pt-BR) as the native language is underserved.** Most apps use English as the explanation language. An app that writes explanations in Portuguese with an LLM (also handling false friends, Spanish↔Portuguese and so on) would stand out.
- **Translation you can trust.** Show the confidence of a translation, offer a human or curated parallel text where one exists, and add a one-tap "why this translation?" LLM explanation. Make it possible to correct a translation.
- **Sync between mobile and desktop** of position, saved words and highlights, since the user reads on both equally. Only LingQ and Ember (Apple-only) do this today.
- **Adaptive bilingual density.** Show more of the native language for hard passages and less as the reader improves, based on known words. Toucan's inline word swap was a version of this, but it never shipped well on mobile.
- **Review that is not a chore.** Fold SRS back into reading by resurfacing saved words inside the next chapter, instead of a separate deck of 171 cards.
- **Cost model.** Pricing that is tied to LLM usage (Readlang's tiers, LinguaRead's 5 free translations a day) frustrates users. Options are on-device models or bring-your-own-key (Ember, LinguaRead) combined with a lifetime unlock.
- **Audio for imported books.** Users can only rely on TTS today, and good neural TTS with word timing is rare outside LingQ Plus and Readlang Plus.

## 4. Sentiment (praise / complaints)
- **Immersive Translate**: praised as the best "read the web bilingually" experience. Firefox rating is **3.7/5 and polarised (306 five-star vs 132 one-star)**, and reviewers frame it as a "translator, not a learning tool": no vocabulary, no SRS (Trancy comparison). Its 2023 move from open source to closed source drew criticism.
- **LingQ**: the free tier is described as "a demo" and the 20-LingQ cap is the top complaint. Other complaints: a steep learning curve, a clunky import workflow (especially on mobile), sentence translation available only on Premium, and imported content deleted on downgrade (LR forum thread).
- **Readlang**: praised as cheap and simple, with good phrase selection and a cloze SRS built from your own reading. Criticised for a dated UI, no audio narration and a shallow review mode for advanced learners.
- **Language Reactor**: loved for Netflix/YouTube. Its reader is weak: **you can't select phrases or collocations**, there is no EPUB import, users call importing into it "monotonous", the UI is confusing, and the AI chat is called "useless".
- **Lute/LWT**: power users love the status colours, keyboard control and zero cost. The downsides are self-hosting, a dated look, no built-in MT and fiddly dictionary setup.
- **Migaku**: useful but "a work in progress" with rough edges; it mostly serves Japanese learners.
- A recurring user wish (LR forum): **read on the original website or book with no import step**, while keeping colour-coding and saving.

## 5. Technical approaches seen
- **Segmentation**: Lute splits by sentence or paragraph and uses language-specific parsers (MeCab for Japanese, jieba-like plugins for Mandarin, Thai). Immersive Translate detects the main content block and translates at paragraph granularity, then injects a sibling node under each paragraph. bilingual_book_maker walks EPUB `<p>` tags and auto-classifies which tags to translate ("plan mode").
- **Alignment**: products mostly avoid true sentence alignment. They pair each paragraph with its translation (interlinear) or show the translation next to the current sentence (LingQ, LR). Readlang aligns at the word/phrase level by translating the selection *in context*. Beelinguapp uses pre-aligned human parallel texts.
- **Context for LLMs**: bilingual_book_maker keeps a session context compacted at 8k tokens so a whole chapter stays consistent, and supports glossaries. Immersive Translate also has glossaries and "personas".
- **Caching/resume**: the Calibre plugin caches every segment, and bilingual_book_maker supports `--resume` and parallel workers per chapter. One full book is translated once and stored. Reading-time lookups are small and cacheable per (lemma, sentence-hash).
- **Engine costs** (simplelocalize 2026):
  - Google NMT costs **$20 per 1M chars** with 500k chars free per month. Microsoft costs **$10 per 1M chars** with 2M free per month. DeepL API Growth costs about **$32.50/mo for 1M chars**.
  - LLMs per 1M tokens (input/output): GPT-4o-mini $0.15/$0.60, Gemini 2.5 Flash $0.30/$2.50, Claude Haiku $1/$5.
  - Rule of thumb: 1k chars is about 250 input + 250 output tokens.
  - A 100k-word novel is about 600k chars. On a mini or flash LLM that is about **$0.10–$1.50 per book**, against about $12 on Google MT.
- **Freemium pattern**: word lookups are free and phrase, sentence or AI calls are metered (Readlang 10/day, LingQ sentence translations Premium-only). This matches the real cost structure: dictionary lookups are cheap and LLM calls are not.

## 6. Desktop-specific UX patterns worth copying
- **Hover beats click** with a mouse: Yomitan's Shift-hover popup, Immersive Translate's paragraph hover, Toucan's hover-to-reveal. Use a modifier key or a hover delay so the cursor resting on text doesn't trigger popups.
- **Click-drag to select a phrase** (Readlang). Language Reactor's lack of this is its most-cited flaw. Also support Shift-click to extend a selection across words.
- **Keyboard-first reading** (Lute and LWT): arrows for page or sentence, `1-5` for word status, `k` for known, `t` to translate the sentence, Space to mark the page read, `?` for help. Desktop learners who read a lot ask for this.
- **Wide screen**:
  - Option A: **two columns**, original | translation, with synced scroll and hover-highlight of the matching sentence. Beelinguapp does this on mobile; nobody does it well on the web.
  - Option B: **interlinear** paragraph-under-paragraph (Immersive Translate). This is the most popular style, but it makes it too easy to read only the translation.
  - Option C: **text plus a persistent right sidebar** for the dictionary, AI explanation and saved-word list (Lute's embedded dictionary frame, LWT's frameset).
- **Progressive reveal**: the translation is blurred or hidden until you hover or press a key. This stops the bilingual overlay from becoming a crutch (the Beelinguapp criticism).
- **Status colouring** (LingQ, Lute, Migaku, LR): it shows the unknown-word density of a page at a glance, and with it whether the text suits your level.
- **Extension plus reader combo**: read in place on any site (Immersive Translate, Migaku), with "send to reader" for long-form reading and EPUB.

## 7. Gaps in the market (desktop / web)
1. **No tool combines Immersive Translate's layout with LingQ or Lute's learning loop.** IT has the best bilingual layout but no vocabulary, status or SRS. LingQ, Lute and Readlang have the loop but weak or no bilingual layouts.
2. **No good web reader for EPUB books with LLM-quality bilingual text.** Today it is batch tools (bilingual_book_maker, the Calibre plugin, both CLI or desktop and non-interactive) or mobile-only apps (Lingible, Linga). Kindle Cloud Reader won't open your own EPUBs.
3. **Context-aware phrase and idiom glossing on desktop with hover and keys.** The AI-explain features are bolted on and metered (Readlang: 10/day).
4. **Adjustable scaffolding**: a per-paragraph or per-user dial from full translation through unknown words only to none, driven by known-word status. Nobody fades the translation automatically as you learn.
5. **Cross-device continuity.** The user splits time between mobile and desktop. Lute is self-hosted, IT keeps no vocabulary, and Kindle has no learning loop. Synced position, statuses and SRS across a web app, an extension and mobile is rare outside LingQ, which is expensive and gated.
6. **Portuguese-native UX (PT-BR glosses and explanations).** Most tools think in English. An LLM can explain grammar in Portuguese and contrast it with Portuguese (false friends, e.g. ES *exquisito*, EN *actually*).
7. **Fair pricing on real costs.** LLM translation of a whole book costs well under $2, yet LingQ charges $15/mo. BYO-key or cache-shared translations of public-domain books could undercut it.
